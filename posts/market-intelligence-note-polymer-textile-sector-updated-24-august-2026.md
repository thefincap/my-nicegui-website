Texsource View by PC

  The polyester chain is firm upstream (crude, PTA, melt) but constrained downstream by demand and price resistance.
  Virgin PSF has gained pricing momentum; rPSF retains a meaningful price advantage but faces tight scrap economics.
  For spinners, the near-term environment remains margin-sensitive, with yarn price realization lagging fiber-cost inflation.
  For weavers, fabric markets continue to show price resistance.
  The polyester value chain is now in a clear upstream-driven price-rise phase: crude-supported PTA and melt costs have translated into fresh PSF hikes (+₹2/kg from 24 Aug), while MEG remains relatively stable.

1. Upstream Market | Firm
  Brent Crude: ~US$89.5/bbl as of 24 August 2026, supported by fading US–Iran peace hopes, reduced tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, and regional supply disruptions.

  Implication: Elevated energy-risk premium keeps PX/PTA/MEG and polyester replacement costs under pressure, supporting upstream firmness into the fiber chain.

2. Price Board | Polyester Chain
  Polyester Intermediates (w.e.f. 22.08.2026)
  Parameter	Price (₹/kg)	Change
  PTA	95.4	+2.4
  MEG	69.7	Steady
  Polyester Melt	105.73	+2.06
  These moves confirm continued upstream cost push from crude and PX/PTA into the polyester chain.

  PSF & Yarn (w.e.f. 24.08.2026)
  Product	Price (₹/kg)	Remarks
  Virgin PSF	~130	+₹2/kg from 24 Aug (from ~₹128/kg mid-August)
  rPSF (OW HT)	92–96	Firm, supported by high PET scrap/flake costs
  Virgin–rPSF Differential	34–38	Attractive for recycled fiber on paper
  15s Spun vPolyester Yarn	~170 (indicative)	Spinners attempting further increases
  Interpretation
  Producers are attempting to protect margins amid elevated crude and energy-risk premiums.
  The wider virgin–rPSF differential enhances the replacement-cost advantage of recycled fiber for brands and converters focused on cost and sustainability.
  However, high PET scrap/flake acquisition costs continue to squeeze recycler margins, limiting how much of this theoretical advantage translates into better realizations.

3. Value-Chain Dynamics
  Flow of cost push: Crude ↑ → PX / PTA / MEG ↑ → Polyester Melt ↑ → PSF (Virgin & Recycled) ↑ → Yarn ↑ → Fabric (partially)

  Upstream firmness is now clearly visible in PTA (+2.4) and melt (+2.06).
  Virgin PSF has been revised up, and rPSF remains firm due to expensive PET scrap and bottle flakes.
  Yarn prices are being pushed up by spinners, but full pass-through is constrained by price-sensitive weavers and fabric makers.
  Demand remains cautious, with buyers deferring large commitments until price direction stabilizes.
  Critical question: Will downstream eventually accept higher yarn/fabric prices, or will spinners and weavers be forced to absorb part of the raw-material inflation through lower margins?

4. Spinner Pulse | Margin Pressure
Key stress points: * Clusters: Surat, Bhiwandi, Ludhiana, Bhilwara – major weaving and processing hubs. * Dynamics: Weavers and fabric manufacturers are resisting full yarn price hikes. Spinners are caught between rising fiber costs and cautious downstream demand and order books. * Result: Compressed spinning margins, especially for those unable to secure timely price increases or hedge raw materials effectively.

5. Texsource Strategic Takeaway
The market is in a transitional phase where upstream cost pushes are clear and ongoing, but downstream acceptance is lagging. Players with better RM hedging, inventory management, and value-added product mixes are likely to navigate this phase with relatively more stable margins.

**📊 The Cost Story Doesn't Fully Add Up: Feedstock Prices Have Softened, Yet Polyester Prices Are Rising**

Crude ↑ | PTA ↓ | MEG → | Polyester Melt ↓ — but PSF ↑ ₹4/kg and Polyester Yarn ↑ ₹4/kg.

The September market therefore warrants closer scrutiny: if upstream replacement costs are stable-to-softer, where is the additional price being created in the polyester chain?

---

1. Upstream Market | Renewed Energy Risk, But Limited Domestic Cost Push

| Parameter | 1 Sep Rate | Trend | Remark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **🛢️ Brent Crude** | ~US$90–92 / bbl | **↑** | Geopolitical / Hormuz risk premium |
| **🧪 PTA** | ₹93.90 / kg | **↓** | Softened by ₹1.50/kg from previous levels |
| **🧪 MEG** | ₹69.70 / kg | **→** | Stable |
| **🏭 Polyester Melt** | ₹104.44 / kg | **↓** | Below recent highs |

Brent has risen above US$90/bbl following renewed US-Iran military escalation and concerns over the Strait of Hormuz. Brent settled at US$90.49/bbl on 31 August, while early September trading moved further higher.

However, the domestic polyester feedstock picture tells a different story. PTA has softened to ₹93.90/kg, MEG remains unchanged at ₹69.70/kg, and polyester melt has eased to ₹104.44/kg as of 31/08/2026.

> **Key Signal: immediate justification for every downstream price increase. The immediate domestic upstream numbers indicate stable-to-softening polyester feedstock costs, even as PSF and yarn prices have been revised upward.

The divergence is becoming increasingly important. PTA and melt have softened, yet PSF has increased ₹4/kg and 15s polyester yarn another ₹4/kg. This suggests that the latest downstream price increases are not simply a mechanical pass-through of current domestic feedstock movements.

Similarly, market reports indicate that Indian polyester raw-material prices eased while downstream polyester prices continued to rise as producers passed through earlier cost increases.

### 💡 TexSource View | Strategic Takeaway by PC

The polyester market appears to be entering a price-discovery phase where producer pricing power is becoming as important as raw-material cost.

Crude has undoubtedly become a risk factor again, particularly with renewed Hormuz disruption concerns. But the latest domestic numbers do not show a corresponding fresh surge in PTA, MEG, or polyester melt. Instead, PTA and melt have actually softened from their recent highs.

**Yet: PSF ↑ ₹4/kg, Yarn ↑ ₹4/kg.** This raises an important industry question:
*When the immediate upstream cost base is stable-to-softer, how much of the downstream price increase represents recovery of earlier costs, and how much reflects producer pricing power and market concentration?*

The concern is particularly relevant in India because the polyester value chain is highly concentrated among a relatively small number of large producers. This does not by itself establish anti-competitive pricing, but it does make the relationship between feedstock costs and finished-fibre prices worthy of closer watch.

The issue has now moved beyond individual buyer discussions. The Southern Gujarat Chamber of Commerce and Industry has approached the Central Government over the sharp rise in polyester yarn prices, highlighting the pressure being felt by Surat's textile industry.

**The rPSF Angle:** At ₹93–97/kg, rPSF remains ₹37–41/kg below Virgin PSF. That is a substantial price differential and strengthens the economic case for recycled fibre wherever quality and application permit substitution. However, high PET scrap/flake costs continue to constrain recycler economics.

The current market is no longer simply a crude-led cost-push story. The widening disconnect between softer feedstock prices and rising PSF/yarn prices points to a deeper question of pricing power, market concentration, and cost pass-through across India's polyester value chain.

For downstream players, the strategic response should therefore be not simply to chase higher prices, but to benchmark actual feedstock costs against producer revisions, monitor spreads, and challenge unjustified cost escalation.
